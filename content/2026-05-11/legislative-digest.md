### Digital Omnibus on AI — formal adoption window opens with the 18–21 May Brussels plenary 🆕 NEW (was: trilogue → now: provisional agreement, awaiting formal adoption)
- **Organiser:** European Parliament (IMCO/LIBE), Council of the EU (Cyprus Presidency)
- **Jurisdiction:** EU
- **Stage:** trilogue (provisional agreement reached 2026-05-07; awaiting formal adoption)
- **Deadline:** 2026-05-21 (end of 18–21 May Brussels plenary; likely first confirmation window)
- **Sources:**
  - https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/05/07/artificial-intelligence-council-and-parliament-agree-to-simplify-and-streamline-rules/
  - https://www.europarl.europa.eu/legislative-train/package-digital-package/file-digital-omnibus-on-ai
  - https://www.nicfab.eu/en/posts/digital-omnibus-ai-deal/
  - https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2026/digital-omnibus-on-ai-provisional-agreement-reached-at-the-may-trilogue
  - https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/05/EUs-Digital-Omnibus-on-AI-7-Key-Changes-You-Need-to-Know
- **Why it matters:** After the 28 April trilogue stalled on Annex I conformity assessment (the file SaferAI does not track most closely), negotiators landed a 7 May provisional deal at 04:30. The five points that matter for SaferAI's frontier-risk agenda: (i) the AI Office retains supervisory competence over AI systems built on a GPAI model where the model and the system come from the same provider or group — the file SaferAI flagged in prior digests is preserved, though with new carve-outs handing competence back to national authorities for law enforcement, border management, judicial authorities and financial institutions; (ii) application dates are locked — 2 December 2027 for stand-alone Annex III systems, 2 August 2028 for Annex I product-embedded systems; (iii) the synthetic-content marking duty applies from 2 December 2026 and, critically, covers systems already on the market before 2 August 2026; (iv) new prohibitions on AI systems generating non-consensual sexually explicit content and CSAM bind both providers and deployers; (v) Annex I machinery is carved out of direct AI Act applicability — health-and-safety obligations now flow via delegated acts under the machinery regulation. Co-legislators have publicly committed to formal adoption before 2 August. The May II plenary is the earliest, June plenary the latest realistic window.

- **Why it matters:** Opened 8 May 2026. ### Colorado AI Act — statutory enforcement date 30 June, with SB 189 rewrite and DOJ-backed litigation now both live 🆕 NEW (was: superseded-in-progress → now: rewrite advancing + federal litigation)
- **Organiser:** Colorado General Assembly (SB 189); US District Court of Colorado / DOJ Civil Rights Division (xAI v. Weiser)
- **Jurisdiction:** US (Colorado / federal)
- **Stage:** amendments (SB 189) + litigation (federal preemption)
- **Deadline:** 2026-06-30 (statutory enforcement date; AG has agreed to delay actual enforcement)
- **Sources:**
  - https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb24-205
  - https://coloradonewsline.com/2026/05/04/narrow-scope-colorado-ai-law/
  - https://www.coloradopolitics.com/2026/05/04/colorado-lawmakers-advance-rewrite-of-2024-law-to-regulate-artificial-intelligence/
  - https://tsscolorado.com/once-controversial-ai-regulatory-bill-gets-unanimous-backing-in-first-committee/
  - https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/justice-department-intervenes-xai-lawsuit-challenging-colorados-algorithmic-discrimination
  - https://www.axios.com/2026/04/24/justice-department-joins-xai-challenge-colorado-ai-law
  - https://statescoop.com/colorado-ag-delaying-ai-law-xai-lawsuit/
- **Why it matters:** Three movements in 14 days, all converging on the 30 June date. (1) **SB 189**, introduced 1 May by Senate President Coleman and Majority Leader Rodriguez (Rodriguez was the lead sponsor of SB24-205), would repeal the existing framework and replace it with a thinner "automated decision-making technology" definition, scrapping affirmative duties to prevent algorithmic discrimination and the upfront-disclosure regime. The bill cleared its first committee unanimously on 4 May. (2) **xAI v. Weiser** (filed 9 April) attacks the Colorado statute on First Amendment, Commerce Clause and Fourteenth Amendment grounds; the **DOJ intervened on 24 April** — the first time the Department has joined a state-AI-law challenge, applying the December 2025 preemption EO operationally. (3) Colorado AG Weiser publicly agreed to suspend enforcement pending the litigation. SaferAI read: the federal-state preemption test SaferAI flagged in the 23 April digest is no longer hypothetical, and a state legislature is now negotiating against the threat of federal litigation rather than the threat of compliance cost. California SB 53 — already in force, with the first incident-reporting window now open — becomes the next plausible target.

## 💡 EDITORIAL NOTE

The Omnibus deal has settled the question SaferAI tracked through the spring: the AI Office keeps supervisory competence over GPAI-derived systems. The political win is real, but the carve-outs — law enforcement, border, judicial, financial — are not cosmetic; they pull exactly the use-cases where systemic-risk evaluations and downstream-deployer obligations would have bitten hardest back into national hands, where supervisory capacity is uneven across Member States. That is the next file. Second-order: with the EU's architecture locked, the centre of gravity for active legal contestation has moved to the United States, where DOJ v. Colorado is now the test that determines whether state safety statutes survive the Trump preemption framework at all. SB 189's progress in Denver is the legislative-side hedge; CADA on 27 May is the EU's compute-side answer to the same trans-Atlantic re-alignment. For SaferAI, the operational priorities are (i) the Article 50 transparency consultation submission (window open 8 May–3 June); (ii) the 15 May energy/emissions filing; (iii) a CADA position before 27 May College; and (iv) tracking the first California SB 53 incident filings as the next plausible federal-preemption target after Colorado.
