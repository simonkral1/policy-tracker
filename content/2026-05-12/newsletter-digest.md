## GDM AI Policy Perspectives

### Good Under the Hood? 🆕 NEW
- **URL:** https://www.aipolicyperspectives.com/p/good-under-the-hood
- **Published:** 2026-05-07
- **Stage:** analysis
- **Why it matters:** Julia Haas and Tom Rachman argue that frontier-model deployments into high-stakes roles (their examples: AI therapists, AI teachers) require evaluations of *moral competence* — the ability to judge novel situations, balance competing factors, and adapt across contexts — and that current post-training pipelines can actively destroy this competence. They flag a case from RLHF where a model overgeneralised certain harms "mechanically", producing what they call moral incompetence as a downstream artefact of safety fine-tuning. The piece is the second consecutive GDM-side argument that frontier evaluation needs to move beyond static capability benchmarks (after the AI-for-science data-stocktake piece on 30 April); for SaferAI it lands directly into the Article 92/101 enforcement-design question of *what* AISI / CAISI / the AI Office should actually be evaluating, and is worth citing in any near-term submission on evaluation-methodology breadth. The accompanying paper, "A roadmap for evaluating moral competence in large language models," is the primary artefact to read.
- **Confidence:** medium
- **Why (confidence):** URL and authorship verified through search; date inferred from search snippet ("recent" / cross-referenced to a 7 May citation in third-party coverage). Substantive claims align with Haas's prior published work on alignment evaluation.

## Hyperdimensional

### Before Leviathan Wakes 🆕 NEW
- **URL:** https://www.hyperdimensional.co/p/before-leviathan-wakes
- **Published:** 2026-05-05
- **Stage:** opinion
- **Why it matters:** Dean Ball's first in-window Hyperdimensional post in five weeks lands the same day CAISI announced its pre-deployment evaluation agreements with Google DeepMind, Microsoft and xAI — and Ball, who drafted America's AI Action Plan inside OSTP before leaving, argues that limited-government conservatives must "own the issue of the state's role in catastrophic-risk mitigation" or the state will monopolise AI for itself. This is the strongest US-right-of-centre argument SaferAI has had for *circumscribed* frontier-risk regulation rather than no regulation — directly useful in any near-term US comparative-governance brief, particularly with Hassett's FDA-style EO reportedly in drafting and Senator Blackburn's competing legislative track active.

## 🔗 Cross-newsletter themes

- **The "prior restraint era" is now the dominant frontier-AI-policy frame on the US side.** Zvi Mowshowitz titles his 7 May roundup "AI #167: The Prior Restraint Era Begins" (https://thezvi.substack.com/p/ai-167-the-prior-restraint-era-begins, secondary newsletter but the only confirmed in-window naming of the shift), and Dean Ball's "Before Leviathan Wakes" (5 May) is the right-of-centre policy argument that locks the frame into the broader debate. The two pieces converge on the same diagnosis from opposite political directions — the state is acquiring frontier-AI vetting authority in real time, and the open question is whether that authority gets bounded by statute (Ball, implicitly Hashim from last week) or operates discretionarily (the current Hassett/CAISI posture). Expect Anton Leicht, Hyperdimensional follow-ups, GDM AI Policy Perspectives, and Tech Policy Press to converge here next week.
- **The trajectory case for capability-based (not FLOP-based) thresholds is hardening fast.** Import AI 456's RSI-economic-growth paper (11 May) builds on 455's automated-AI-R&D essay (4 May, covered last week); Epoch's new chip-supply-chain data explorer (Brief, 8 May) flags HBM not raw FLOPs as the binding constraint; and the CAISI evaluations themselves are capability-focused (more than 40 completed to date, per the agency's own framing). For any SaferAI submission on the AI Act systemic-risk methodology or on US CAISI authorising legislation, the strongest currently-available evidence base for moving beyond FLOPs is now this triad — RSI economics + HBM bottleneck + CAISI capability evaluations.
- **"Radical optionality" is the framing fight to engage now.** Jack Clark's pointed critique of the Institute for Law & AI's framework — that transparency requirements, whistleblower protections and third-party auditing are "the basics" of any regulated industry, not a regulatory posture — is the first serious frontier-adjacent pushback on what could otherwise become the default safety-policy-adjacent pitch to US Congressional staff and EU Article 56 GPAI code-of-practice signatories. If SaferAI does not engage on the framing, the ceiling on "serious frontier-risk regulation" risks being set by a framework Clark already calls "closer to a posture than a policy."
