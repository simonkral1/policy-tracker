### Colorado SB 26-189 — bill on Governor Polis's desk; Colorado AI Act repeal-and-replace imminent 🆕 NEW (was: amendments advancing → now: passed both chambers, awaiting signature)
- **Organiser:** Colorado General Assembly (SB 26-189); Governor Jared Polis
- **Jurisdiction:** US (Colorado)
- **Stage:** adopted (legislature); awaiting executive signature
- **Deadline:** 2026-05-22 (indicative signing window, 10 days from concurrence vote)
- **Sources:**
  - https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb26-189
  - https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2026/05/colorado-legislature-passes-bill-to-repeal-and-replace-colorado-ai-act/
  - https://coloradosun.com/2026/05/12/colorado-ai-law-rewrite-passes/

  - https://www.bakerbotts.com/thought-leadership/publications/2026/may/colorado-repeals-and-replaces-ai-act
- **Why it matters:** SB 26-189 cleared the Senate 34–1 and the House 57–6, with the Senate concurring in House technical amendments on 12 May. The bill repeals SB 24-205 in full and replaces it with a thinner ADMT (automated decision-making technology) framework — disclosure-based, with narrow consumer rights and no affirmative duty to prevent algorithmic discrimination, no risk-management programme, no impact-assessment regime. Effective 1 January 2027; AG rulemaking begins on the signature date. Polis was a driving force in the rewrite and is expected to sign within the constitutional window. Two implications SaferAI should track: (i) the gold-standard upfront-safeguards model that SB 24-205 attempted is now gone — what replaces it is closer to the federal-framework-compatible posture the White House preemption EO is pushing other states toward; (ii) signature triggers xAI v. Weiser's 28-day amendment window (see ON THE RADAR), reactivating the federal litigation track on the new statute rather than the old one. California SB 53's catastrophic-incident reporting regime is now the only state-level frontier-AI obligation still in force.

  - https://www.handleygill.co.uk/handley-gill-blog/kings-speech-2026-parliament-legislation
- **Why it matters:** The 13 May King's Speech delivered the long-awaited government answer to "what comes after the 2023 White Paper" — and the answer is no standalone AI bill. The headline AI item is the **Regulating for Growth Bill**: cross-economy sandboxing powers giving the executive authority to temporarily relax existing sectoral rules in controlled environments, including explicit "cross-cutting AI sandboxes" for testing AI-enabled products and services across multiple sectors. Sector regulators (Ofcom, CMA, ICO, FCA, MHRA) keep their AI remits; the bill provides coordination, common definitions, and statutory backing for AISI's role — the first time AISI's existence and evaluation function would be set out in primary legislation. Lord Holmes's Artificial Intelligence (Regulation) Bill [HL] remains the only private-member vehicle for a more comprehensive frontier-AI regime; the King's Speech architecture suggests the government will not adopt that approach. The bill sits inside a wider package — Cyber Security and Resilience Bill (carried over from 2024–26 session, broadens reporting to managed IT services and data centres), Computer Misuse Act reform, and a Financial Services Bill. Two implications SaferAI should track: (i) statutory cover for AISI is a tangible governance win — the empirical work documented across the Mythos, sabotage and Microsoft-partnership tracks now has a legislative anchor; (ii) the absence of a frontier-AI statutory regime leaves the UK without an Article 92-equivalent supervisory power, which is the exact gap SaferAI has flagged in prior digests, now durable until the next King's Speech.
- **Confidence:** medium
- **Why (confidence):** Bill text not yet published; first-reading timetable inferred from session programming, not confirmed.

## 💡 EDITORIAL NOTE

The week's structural read is that two long-running ambiguities resolved at once, and the resolution in both cases was a step down in regulatory ambition. London chose a sandboxing-and-coordination model over a frontier-AI statutory regime; Denver chose a disclosure framework over the upfront-safeguards model it pioneered in 2024. Each looks, from SaferAI's vantage, like an asymmetric loss — the Mythos-era empirical case for binding pre-deployment supervision has been at its strongest exactly in the months the UK and one of the two state laboratories of US AI safety legislation stepped back from it. The compensating fact is that the EU architecture, which the May 7 Omnibus deal preserved, is now the only major jurisdiction with statutory model-access powers about to come into force, with three contested implementation files (Article 50 transparency, the high-risk classification rewrite, CADA) all converging on the May–June calendar. SaferAI's operational priorities for the next 14 days are correspondingly narrow and overlapping: a 27 May CADA position framing safe-by-design conditionality on compute; a 3 June Article 50 submission tying the deepfake-deployer duty to systemic-risk supervision; and a watching brief on Polis's signature and the resulting xAI v. Weiser amendment, which will be the test of whether the federal preemption theory bites even a disclosure-only state regime.
