### The campaign to stop federal AI laws is backfiring 🆕 NEW
- **URL:** https://www.transformernews.ai/p/the-campaign-to-stop-federal-ai-laws-illinois-sb-315
- **Published:** 2026-05-28 (approximate; post-dates the 27 May Illinois House vote and immediately precedes the 28 May Anthropic Series H)
- **Stage:** analysis
- **Why it matters:** Shakeel Hashim's lead argues the Andreessen Horowitz / American Innovators Network campaign to head off state AI laws via federal pre-emption has produced the opposite of the intended result: Illinois SB 315 — described as the country's strictest state AI safety law to date — passed the House 110-0 on 27 May (Senate previously 21 May), Governor Pritzker has publicly committed to signing it, and the longer Washington fails to legislate the higher the state-level floor that any future federal pre-emption attempt will need to clear (https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/illinois-legislature-passes-historic-ai-bill-rcna347191). The bill stacks California SB 53 and New York's RAISE Act transparency requirements with a *third-party safety audit* mandate (in-force 1 January 2027), plus whistleblower protections — and Build American AI, the 501(c)(4) arm of the OpenAI / a16z-backed Leading the Future super PAC, came out in support *after* it passed, in tension with the American Innovators Network's opposition. For SaferAI: the third-party safety-audit mandate is the *first* binding US instrument that maps cleanly to the AI Act Article 56 CoP third-party-evaluation thread; the Illinois implementation rulemaking (now Q3-Q4 2026) is the natural US-side companion file to any EU Article 92 evaluation-design submission, and the political-economy reading — that statutory frontier-AI safety regulation now has demonstrably more political room in 2026 than the federal-pre-emption camp had assumed — is the strongest evidence base yet for the case SaferAI has been making since the 5 May digest.

### What the Pope got wrong 🆕 NEW
- **URL:** https://www.transformernews.ai/p/what-the-pope-got-wrong-leo-ai-encyclical-catholic-church-ai-magnifica-humanitas
- **Published:** 2026-05-26 (approximate; published in the days immediately after the 25 May release of Magnifica Humanitas, https://www.vatican.va/content/leo-xiv/en/encyclicals/documents/20260515-magnifica-humanitas.html)
- **Stage:** opinion
- **Why it matters:** Hashim's critique of Pope Leo XIV's first encyclical (Magnifica Humanitas, signed 15 May, released 25 May, 245 paragraphs / 42,300 words, presented at the Vatican alongside Anthropic co-founder Christopher Olah) is that the document reads "like an AI ethics paper from 2022": its focus on algorithmic bias, water use, data sovereignty, autonomous weapons and mass surveillance is correctly identified but is not the front of the frontier-risk argument in 2026. Hashim concedes the document gets the political-economy framing right — it criticises "the race for ever more powerful algorithms and larger datasets, driven by the desire to secure geopolitical or commercial dominance" — but argues the encyclical's failure to engage with transformative AI capability trajectories ultimately limits its policy traction. For SaferAI: the encyclical is the single highest-profile religious-moral intervention on AI yet published, and its framing (concentration of power, human dignity, "AI must be disarmed") will be cited by EU MEPs, US Senate Democrats, and Latin American governments through Q3 — worth a position-piece by SaferAI's policy lead that engages the encyclical's correct political-economy frame while pushing it forward to the actual frontier-capability discussion the document declines to enter.

## Hyperdimensional

### A Cascade of Conscientiousness 🆕 NEW
- **URL:** https://www.hyperdimensional.co/p/a-cascade-of-conscientiousness
- **Published:** 2026-05-28
- **Stage:** analysis
- **Why it matters:** Dean Ball's first Hyperdimensional post since "Aviate, Navigate, Communicate" (covered in the 19 May digest with date uncertainty) pivots away from frontier-model policy and into *physical AI* — the policy framework for robotic and autonomous-system deployment, which Ball argues is the next file frontier-risk policy will need to engage. He proposes a three-bucket framework: (1) one-to-one human-labour replacements (self-driving taxis); (2) industries fundamentally reimagined by autonomy (autonomous-drone delivery); (3) wholly novel tasks that only autonomy enables (robotic Lunar infrastructure construction). The "cascade of conscientiousness" framing is the policy claim — that as physical AI proliferates in domains hostile to human biology (advanced manufacturing, robotic surgery) the regulatory burden shifts from compliance-with-existing-rules to *new rule-creation in domains that don't yet have rules*. For SaferAI: this is not the immediate Q3 2026 file, but it is the structural framing the EU AI Act systemic-risk methodology will need by 2027 — physical-AI deployment is the first major domain where the AI Act's existing high-risk Annex III categories were drafted without the autonomous-system capabilities they will need to govern, and Ball's framework is the most coherent right-of-centre policy frame in circulation. Worth reading before any AI Act biennial-review submission.

For SaferAI: Leigh's speech is the first time a sitting G20 finance/treasury minister has made the cost-benefit-pricing argument SaferAI's own research stream has been making since 2024, and it dramatically lowers the political cost of citing extinction-risk framings in technical-Treasury and OECD-level work. ### The Epoch Brief — June 1, 2026 🆕 NEW
- **URL:** https://epochai.substack.com/p/the-epoch-brief-june-1-2026
- **Published:** 2026-06-01
- **Stage:** reported
- **Why it matters:** Three data points that will define the compute-governance conversation in Q3 2026. (1) **The compute crunch is now measured, not predicted**: Luke Emberson and Jaime Sevilla's lead Gradient Update models global inference capacity and finds **token supply growing 3-4× per year, token demand growing ~10× per year** — a measured gap that "suggests a compute crunch is nearing, if not already here." This is the first publicly-quantified version of the argument SaferAI has been hearing in private conversations since the 22 May Brief flagged HBM as the binding constraint. (2) **Open-weight models lag the closed frontier by four months** as of January 2026, with the gap *widening slightly* since October 2025; Epoch puts the average performance differential at 8 points, comparable to GPT-5 → GPT-5.5 — useful primary citation for the GDM Kimi K2.5 argument above, and directly relevant to any AI Act Article 51 open-source-exception discussion (the lag is shrinking by quarter, not stable). (3) **Hyperscaler capex on trajectory to $770bn in 2026, >$1tn in 2027** — Q1 2026 hyperscaler spending came in on trend, and capex has quadrupled since GPT-4. For SaferAI: the demand-vs-supply gap (3-4× vs 10×) and the four-month open-weight lag are the two single most-citable data points the policy team will need this quarter; the $770bn capex projection is the political-economy companion that any G7 export-control or AI Act Article 56 CoP discussion will run into. The framing of "compute crunch" as a measured (not anticipated) condition is the rhetorical shift to track — it changes the burden of proof from "will scarcity bind?" to "how is scarcity allocated?", which is exactly the Anton Leicht "Cut Off" argument that landed two digests ago.

## EU AI Act Newsletter

### #103: The August Countdown 🆕 NEW
- **URL:** https://artificialintelligenceact.substack.com/p/the-eu-ai-act-newsletter-103-the
- **Published:** 2026-06-01
- **Stage:** analysis
- **Why it matters:** Risto Uuk's first issue after #102 (20 May) lays out the **2 August 2026 enforcement-power activation** in operational detail — and this is the single highest-priority near-term file SaferAI is tracking on the EU side. Three concrete elements. (1) **Articles 91-93 enforcement powers**: the AI Office can (Art 91) demand technical documentation, training-data summaries and model reports; (Art 92) commission independent evaluations; and (Art 93) require mitigation measures or *restrict/withdraw/recall* a model from the market — backed by Article 101 fines of up to **3% of global annual turnover** for failures to respond to information or evaluation requests. Uuk's framing: "technical compliance dialogues" remain the AI Office's primary tool and will "intensify after August", with formal powers reserved for cases where dialogues prove insufficient. (2) The Commission has opened a **targeted consultation on draft guidelines for classifying high-risk AI systems**, running until **23 June 2026** — a hard deadline SaferAI's EU-policy track should plan a written submission against. (3) The AI Office published a **GPAI FAQ** clarifying GPAI definitions, systemic-risk classification, compute-threshold interactions, provider-identity rules, open-source obligations, energy-consumption documentation, serious-incident reporting and the GPAI Code of Practice; **Poland's parliamentary Committee on Digitalization** adopted a draft AI bill aligning national rules with the AI Act, establishing a "Commission for the Development and Security of Artificial Intelligence" as the national oversight body. For SaferAI: the **23 June high-risk-classification consultation deadline** is the next concrete EU-side filing moment and should be the focus of next week's planning; the Article 92 independent-evaluation power is the natural attachment point for the SaferAI argument (now bolstered by the GDM Kimi K2.5 piece above) that systemic-risk evaluations must be *open-weight-uplift-aware*, not just FLOP-threshold-aware.
