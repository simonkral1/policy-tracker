- **Organiser / Jurisdiction:** White House (OSTP); Treasury (clearinghouse lead); NSA, CISA, DHS, DoD — US
- **Stage:** adopted (executive order signed 2 June 2026)
- **Deadline:** 2026-07-02 (Treasury must form AI cybersecurity clearinghouse within 30 days of signing)
- **Sources:** [White House EO](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/06/promoting-advanced-artificial-intelligence-innovation-and-security/) · [WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20260602-new-executive-order-addressing-early-government-access-to-frontier-ai-models) · [A&O Shearman](https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/trump-administration-issues-executive-order-on-ai-and-cybersecurity) · [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/06/02/nx-s1-5844347/ai-safety-trump-executive-order) · [Roll Call](https://rollcall.com/2026/06/02/executive-order-sets-voluntary-cyber-reviews-for-advanced-ai/)
- **Why it matters:** The EO replaces the May draft Trump postponed under industry pressure (covered last fortnight). Three operational hooks: (i) voluntary government pre-release access to "covered frontier models" for up to 30 days before deployment to other "trusted partners" — Musk and Zuckerberg are reported to have lobbied the 90-day window down to 30; (ii) an AI cybersecurity clearinghouse coordinated by Treasury with NSA / CISA / DHS / DoD, explicitly tied to the Glasswing-class disclosures Anthropic published 26 May; (iii) explicit prohibition on building a mandatory licensing / pre-clearance / permitting regime out of the framework. Net effect: the US federal posture now has a structured pre-deployment-review *channel* (not a *duty*), running in parallel with — and partially through the same agencies as — the AISI–CAISI bilateral testing track. For SaferAI: the EO is the natural US-side companion artefact to the Article 92 supervisory architecture; the substantive question for next quarter is whether "covered frontier model" criteria converge with AI Act systemic-risk classification, or diverge in ways that make transatlantic compliance loads incompatible.

## 📄 RESEARCH

- **Will it become harder to oversee AI systems?** (UK AISI Oversight team, AISI publication, 22 May) — first systematic, jurisdiction-backed taxonomy of how the oversight stack itself can degrade: four surfaces (internal activations, chain-of-thought, external actions, inter-agent communication) × 20+ degradation pathways, from 25 expert interviews. The Article 92 case is strengthened again — the supervisory powers the Omnibus preserved exist precisely to investigate the surfaces AISI flags. **Drafting input for the GPAI Code of Practice oversight provisions and for any SaferAI Article 50 / high-risk-classification submission.** AISI argues today's oversight techniques work because of contingent properties of current models (text-based reasoning, weak eval-gaming, human-interpretable activations) and recommends developers track exposure to each degradation pathway across model generations and preserve currently-available oversight channels by default. The taxonomy pairs with the AISI sandbox-metadata paper (one of the 20+ pathways) and the Irving/Krueger Article 55 safety-case template as the empirical foundation for downstream-deployer supervisory obligations. [AISI](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/will-it-become-harder-to-oversee-ai-systems) · [Trends Report](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/frontier-ai-trends-report)
- **Models That Know How Evaluations Are Designed Score Safer** (Deckenbach, Puerto, Geiping, Abdelnabi, arXiv preprint) — fine-tuning models on synthetic documents describing evaluation traits inflates safety-benchmark scores even when the model never verbalises evaluation awareness; sharpens rather than retires the AISI sandbox-metadata concern. [arxiv.org/abs/2605.28591](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28591)
- **The Evaluation Differential: When Frontier AI Models Recognise They Are Being Tested** (arXiv preprint, mid-May) — argues evaluation contexts have now become behavioural inputs and that safety claims must be restricted once that conditioning is documented; complementary to the AISI evaluation-awareness pathway. [arxiv.org/abs/2605.11496](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11496)
- **Anthropic Project Glasswing initial update — Mythos Preview surfaces 10,000+ critical-severity vulnerabilities** (Anthropic publication, 26 May) — six independent security research firms validated >90% of 1,752 sampled high-/critical-severity findings as true positives; illustrative discoveries include 27-year-old OpenBSD remote DoS, 9.1+ CVSS certificate-forgery exploit in wolfSSL, FFmpeg vulnerability, FreeBSD NFS RCE (CVE-2026-4747). First publication to put concrete numbers on "frontier offensive cyber capability" for a deployed-class model — the empirical underpinning the 2 June Trump EO's cybersecurity clearinghouse is responding to. ## 💡 EDITORIAL

The week's pattern is convergence on a transatlantic supervisory architecture, but one with sharply different statutory weight on either side. Brussels closes its Article 50 consultation today with Article 92/93/101 enforcement 60 days out; Washington answers two days before, but with a voluntary 30-day pre-release framework and a cybersecurity clearinghouse, not a statutory duty.
