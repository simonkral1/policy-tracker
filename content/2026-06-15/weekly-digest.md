## 📄 RESEARCH

The kind of paper the Scientific Panel will need to absorb early, since the political mileage of recent Anthropic / Apollo deception findings depends on the answer. (confidence: medium — surfaced via search snippet; authors not independently verified.) [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08629)
- **Emergent Misalignment Can Be Induced by Sycophancy and Reversed via Alignment Gating** (arXiv 2606.09068, June 2026) — Direct follow-up to the Evans et al. emergent-misalignment thread; identifies sycophancy fine-tuning as a driver of broad misalignment and proposes Alignment Gating as a mitigation. Useful citation for the argument that AI Office evaluation protocols must include training-pipeline audits, not only behavioural tests. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09068)

[arxiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05391)

## 💡 EDITORIAL

The asymmetry of the week is between asks: a frontier lab on 10 June asked Washington for FAA-style binding pre-release authority over its own models; the White House is reported the same week negotiating with Senator Blackburn to disable state-level binding regulation in exchange for child-safety wins; the 4 June Great American AI Act discussion draft gives that preemption track its first serious bipartisan House vehicle. Strasbourg's Omnibus formal-adoption window opens 48 days before AI Act enforcement, and the first ever US "block-deployment" order against a deployed frontier system (Mythos, 12-13 June) reaches into actor-by-actor enforcement two months before the EU has the legal authority to do the same. The political question for the second half of 2026 is no longer whether frontier-AI supervision happens — the second EU CoP is adopted, the Scientific Panel is seated with public DoIs, the external-evaluator workshop is on the calendar — but whether the binding-rule camp on either side of the Atlantic can hold the line against a US preemption track that has now found its bipartisan vehicle.
