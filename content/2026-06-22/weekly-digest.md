## 💡 EDITORIAL

The cycle's centre of gravity moved this week — not on Article 92 supervision (the EU machinery did exactly what was planned: EP-adopted the Omnibus and left the GPAI-systemic-risk text untouched) but on what selective access does to alliance politics. The single most consequential fact of the cycle is that the most-capable lab model on the market is offline worldwide today because the US Commerce Department exercised an export authority that, as of two weeks ago, had no public playbook, and that Macron and Carney are now publicly arguing against from the same room where Amodei and Hassabis proposed a US-led democratic-AI coalition. The Mythos directive is producing the EU sovereign-cloud political coalition that the Commission's CADA proposal needed and could not have produced on its own. The Blackburn package's quiet pivot — from blanket preemption (Great American AI Act discussion draft) to a child-safety-vehicle with limited AI carve-outs (KOSA + NO FAKES + age-verification + "limited subject-area preemption") — is the corresponding political-economy adjustment on the US side: a harder coalition to oppose, but one whose preemption clause now arrives after the federal executive has, in practice, demonstrated a deployment-control power that no statute grants. The question for SaferAI through July is no longer whether selective-deployment regimes exist — they do, twice this fortnight, once via export controls and once via the Pentagon supply-chain track — but whether the EU's Scientific Panel and external-evaluator workshop can produce a more transparent version of that authority before the August enforcement powers activate against a frontier-model market in which the leader is, this morning, not on the market at all.
