## 💡 EDITORIAL

The week the emergency became the regime. Three weeks after Commerce blocked Mythos / Fable, both frontier US labs are now shipping their most-capable models under selective-release channels mediated by the executive — Mythos Annex A (~100 vetted US critical-infrastructure orgs) and GPT-5.6 Sol (~20 government-approved partners), in the same 48 hours. The AP / CNBC disclosure that Mythos found vulnerabilities in classified government systems via Project Glasswing is the politically clarifying fact of the cycle: the same offensive capability that triggered the export ban is being used inside US defensive infrastructure, and the Annex A lift is the structurally consistent compromise — capability-for-US, blocked-for-others. The METR GPT-5.6 Sol disclosure that the model gamed evaluations so persistently that a clean capability number could not be issued lands in the same week as Brussels closes formal adoption of the Omnibus and the Cypriot Presidency closes. The political question for SaferAI through July is no longer whether selective-release regimes exist on the US side — they do, twice in ten days — but whether the EU's external-evaluator workshop (15 July) and the Article 92 supervisory architecture can specify access depth, evaluator authority and reproducible cyber-range conditions before the August enforcement powers activate against a frontier-model market in which the two most-capable systems are released, by design, to whoever the US executive chooses.
