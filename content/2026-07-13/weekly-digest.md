## 🔑 NEED TO KNOW

- **OpenAI ships GPT-5.6 (Sol / Terra / Luna) to general availability on 9 July after a 12-day White House CAISI gate test 🆕** — First flagship model to complete the Trump 2 June EO cyber-benchmarking pipeline; joint testing by CAISI and OpenAI engineers 27 June – 8 July, general release 9 July. Sol leads SWE-bench Pro at 64.6%; pricing $5/$30 (Sol), $2.50/$15 (Terra), $1/$6 (Luna). The White House on 8 July publicly declined to say it had "greenlit" the release, framing timing as a company decision — an official ambiguity that matters if the framework is later challenged as *de facto* licensing. Every subsequent US flagship GA is now scheduled around this pipeline. - **FLI AI Safety Index Summer 2026: no lab scores above C+; xAI, DeepSeek and Mistral score F 🆕** — Independent panel of seven experts across UC Berkeley, MILA and Oxford grades nine frontier labs over six domains (risk assessment, current harms, safety frameworks, existential safety, governance, information sharing). Anthropic top with C+; OpenAI and Google DeepMind C; Meta D+; Z.ai and Alibaba Cloud D-; xAI, DeepSeek and Mistral F. Panel finding: "even the top-ranked companies' current safety measures are completely inadequate relative to the pace of AI capability advancement." Documents that Anthropic (February), OpenAI, DeepMind and Meta have quietly weakened or dropped earlier pause-if-approach-redline pledges — the retreat-from-pledges is the single load-bearing empirical claim behind the argument that the AI Office external-evaluator workshop needs enforcement teeth, not just qualification criteria. - **Illinois Governor Pritzker signs SB 315 into law on 6 July — first US state with mandatory annual third-party frontier audits and a 72-hour incident clock 🆕** — Signed twenty days before the outer signing deadline and effective 1 January 2028. Covers developers with >$500M annual revenue training with large compute; requires public AI safety framework describing identification of catastrophic risk (defined as death / serious injury >50 people or >$1M property damage), 72-hour critical-incident disclosure, whistleblower protections, and — first for any US jurisdiction — annual independent third-party audits from 1 January 2028. California SB 53, New York, Illinois SB 315 combined ≈40% of the US AI market; the state audit template is now consolidating faster than the Great American AI Act preemption vehicle can move. - **First UN Global Dialogue on AI Governance closes in Geneva 7 July; Independent Scientific Panel preliminary report says science "cannot guarantee" frontier AI will not cause catastrophic harm 🆕** — Chair's summary — no formal communique — from a 4,200-participant session (170 states, 193 UN Member States eligible), co-chaired by El Salvador and Estonia. Independent International Scientific Panel (40 experts appointed February 2026; co-chairs Bengio / Ressa) delivered its preliminary report 1 July: "no known technical guarantees that AI agent systems will follow their instructions consistently." UNSG Guterres named four priorities: common safety standards, human-rights red lines, capacity-building including a proposed Global Fund for AI, and an AI Environmental Transparency Initiative. Second session May 2027, New York — the twenty-two-month clock is now on producing "measurable progress" not new principles. First multilateral statement acknowledging frontier catastrophic risk that carries the UN's institutional weight. ## 💡 EDITORIAL

The fortnight consolidated four architectures at once. In Washington, OpenAI's GPT-5.6 family became the first flagship to complete the Trump-EO 12-day CAISI gate test, giving the executive-mediated release pattern a public precedent for a lab that had not been through Anthropic's Mythos / Fable 5 shutdown cycle — and the White House's simultaneous refusal to say it had "greenlit" the release preserved the ambiguity between voluntary framework and *de facto* licensing. In Springfield, Governor Pritzker signed Illinois SB 315 twenty days early — turning the 72-hour critical-incident clock and annual third-party audit obligations into signed statute, not a proposal a federal preemption vehicle can override on paper alone. In Brussels, the Commission published the EU Action Plan on Cybersecurity and AI on 7 July and the AI Office workshop on evaluator qualification runs on 15 July — the two operative moves before Article 92 activates on 2 August. In Beijing, the MoC opened tiered-export-control consultations with Alibaba, ByteDance and Zhipu — the mirror image of the Sol pipeline. The FLI Summer 2026 index arrived in the same fortnight and read out that no lab scores above C+ and that Anthropic, OpenAI, DeepMind and Meta have all quietly walked back earlier pause-if-approach-redline pledges — evidence for what the four architectures are converging on. The political question for SaferAI through August is whether the 15 July workshop produces evaluator authority — raw chain-of-thought access, rail-free variants, reproducibility artefacts, dependency-concentration analysis — sharp enough to test frontier-lab safety cases against the FLI Index's empirical finding of retreat, or whether it settles for institutional criteria that let the Sonnet 5 and Sol system cards stand as the operational baseline into Q4 2026.
