## 🔑 NEED TO KNOW

- **AI Act Article 92 / 93 / 101 GPAI enforcement powers activated Sunday 2 August; no launch RFI, no new guidance document, no first-day designation announced 🆕** — After a one-year adjustment period since the 2 August 2025 substantive obligations, the AI Office can now issue formal information requests, compel model access for evaluation, order corrective measures, restrict or withdraw models from the EU market, and impose fines up to €15M or 3% of global turnover (Article 101). The Office signalled it would "treat signatories as acting in good faith through the transition" while intending to "fully enforce GPAI requirements from 2 August, signatures or not." The final formal EC output before activation was the 20 July Article 50 transparency guidelines; the Marking & Labelling initial signatory list closed 27 July at ~190 signatories. Silence on Day 1 is itself a supervisory choice — Article 92 is now a live instrument. ## 💡 EDITORIAL

The week Article 92 went live was the week the government side went quiet and the labs did the talking. Brussels activated the enforcement instrument, published no first-day RFI, named no first designation, and — via the 20 July Article 50 guidelines and the 27 July Marking & Labelling signatory close — chose a supervisory posture of restrained readiness. Washington missed all three EO 14409 deliverables on 1 August, and Cruz on 29 July pulled the preemption vehicle off the summer calendar. Into that vacuum arrived the Pacing the Frontier letter — 1,268 employees, Anthropic and OpenAI corporately endorsing within hours, Amodei and Pachocki and Zhao and Dragan among signatories — explicitly asking the US government to build the mechanism to slow AI development *before* it's needed, on Anthropic's own empirical claim of ~52x code-optimisation speedup and 97% weak-to-strong AI-safety-research recovery. The joint UK AISI / US CAISI Kimi K3 assessment — step 17 versus 28.5, safeguards not preventing offensive-cyber assistance on a permanently-downloadable model — is the empirical exhibit the AI Office will now have to reason about in its first Article 92 designation cycle, and the FBI Cyber Division's 28 July industrial-scale-exploitation warning is the parallel US law-enforcement statement of the same problem. The political question through August is whether the AI Office uses its enforcement week of choice on Kimi K3's documented safeguards gap or on Opus 5's cyber-training omission methodology, or holds fire until the CoE Convention enters into force and the CoP Safety & Security signatories are on the record — but the empirical case for evaluator authority is now the strongest it has ever been at exactly the moment the government instruments that could exercise it have chosen either restraint or delay.
