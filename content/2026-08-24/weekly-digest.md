## 🔑 NEED TO KNOW

- **OpenAI escalates Astra pause into a formal Preparedness Framework disclosure (18 August): first frontier model ever assessed as potentially reaching the 'Critical' cybersecurity threshold; largest planned RL run held; universal monitoring imposed; external evaluation by government agencies invited 🆕** — What began on 7 August as a quiet Axios line about slowed development became a full Preparedness Framework escalation this week. OpenAI states it "cannot rule out" that Astra reaches the Critical cybersecurity level, which under the December 2023 framework requires a model that can "identify and develop functional zero-day exploits of all severity levels in many hardened real-world critical systems without human intervention" or "devise and execute end-to-end novel strategies for cyberattacks against hardened targets given only a high-level desired goal." Astra is the first model in company history to reach this branch of the framework. OpenAI paused internal activities without safeguards, imposed universal action monitoring across all uses of Astra (training and evaluation), sandboxed development environments with limited network connectivity, and invited government agencies and AI safety organisations to jointly test capabilities. Preliminary assessment; final classification pending. ## 💡 EDITORIAL

Three weeks of the enforcement era, three weeks in which the empirical warrant for evaluator authority has been extended by covered providers rather than by the regulator. Week one gave the AISI incident. Week two gave the Anthropic Risk Report — misalignment upgraded, threshold benchmark saturated, biosafety classifier disabled for eleven months. Week three has given two more: OpenAI's Astra Preparedness Framework escalation, the first-ever pre-deployment "cannot rule out Critical" assessment in a frontier company's own governance record, and Anthropic's Claude autonomous protein-binder result, the first end-to-end AI drug design at industrial evaluation scale by a covered GPAI-with-systemic-risk provider. Both arrived without any Article 92(4) proceeding. Washington's decision to publish an S&T strategy that ranks AI + autonomy first and mentions open-weight AI nowhere completes the political frame: an operational federal open-weight programme running under a strategic document that refuses to name open-weight AI, in the same seven days OpenAI describes its own frontier system as potentially Critical for cyber. The transatlantic policy divergence is now a two-document divergence: Article 92 armed and unused, NSSTS specific and comprehensive-except-open-weight. The CoE Framework Convention enters into force for the EU eight days from now. California's session adjourns seven days from now. The next Article 92 move — first RFI or first designation — will now be read against a fact pattern in which two US covered providers, one UK national institute, and one EU-legally-binding treaty have all inserted themselves into the record while the AI Office has remained the only party in a supervisory posture that has not moved.
