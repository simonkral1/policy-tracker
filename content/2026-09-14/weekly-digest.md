## 💡 EDITORIAL

Week six was the week the concessions arrived, and they came from inside the room. A covered-lab CEO conceded pace-the-frontier; three peer CEOs agreed on the record within hours; two covered labs committed to permanent employee-level third-party evaluator access; a covered-lab pretraining researcher resigned and reached 76 million viewers with 'gambling with our lives'; a serving alignment lead publicly priced >10% extinction over the decade; a covered lab filed its first Article 55 incident report and admitted a monitoring gap; a US governor signed the first independent-AI-audit statute; and a US Senate committee prepared to convert voluntary Preparedness language into a legal duty — inside seven days. The transatlantic-supervisory-divergence reading of the prior five weeks was that the enforcement architecture was moving faster than the labs. The week-six reading is that the labs' own commitments are now the fastest-moving artefacts on the record, and the question for the AI Office, for Commerce, for the Signatory Taskforce, and for SaferAI is no longer whether covered-lab commitments exist to reason against — it is whether the supervisory instruments can catch what the labs themselves have publicly conceded before the pledge decays.
