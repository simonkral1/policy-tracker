## 📄 RESEARCH

- **"Independent assessment of AI R&D acceleration at Anthropic"** (METR, 5 Nov, report) — This is the first high-access outside measurement of whether AI is accelerating AI research inside a frontier lab, and the answer is probably yes. [METR](https://metr.org/blog/2026-11-05-anthropic-ai-rd-acceleration-assessment) · [LessWrong summary](https://lesswrong.com/posts/N5AmYkRks5CqAxvBg/metr-independent-assessment-of-ai-r-and-d-acceleration-at) 🆕

  METR staff worked on-site at Anthropic from 12 August to 29 October. They estimate that capability gains on well-defined tasks are now about twice as fast as from March 2024 to October 2025, and coverage cites a figure of 1.9x (95% CI 1.5–2.8x). They attribute most of that to Anthropic's internal use of AI rather than to added compute, though Anthropic staff did not agree on this. The more important point for policy is that Anthropic redacted most of the evidence METR found most informative, so the public has to rely on METR's judgement more than METR itself thinks is ideal. Embedded access produced the finding, but redaction rights limited what could be verified. That makes the case for regulators (the AI Office, New York's proposed examiners) to get unredacted access by law rather than by agreement with the lab. `(confidence: medium — figures via secondary coverage; full ~100pp report not fetched)`
- **"Trust but Verify: Implementing Frontier AI Policy in California and Beyond"** (Carnegie Endowment, Nov 2026, report) — This follow-up by the authors of the California Report focuses on how to implement SB 53, which New York and Illinois have now copied. It arrives as a Democratic Congress makes SB 53-style transparency the likely federal baseline. [Carnegie](https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2026/11/trust-but-verify-implementing-frontier-ai-policy-in-california-and-beyond) `(confidence: medium — summary only)` 🆕
- **Bayesian Epoch Capabilities Index** (Epoch AI, 4 Nov, methodology report) — The index now comes with uncertainty estimates and head-to-head probabilities; Epoch puts GPT-6 Astra's chance of beating Opus 5.5 at 72%. Laws and frameworks that trigger obligations at a "frontier" rank, such as China's top-ten test, now have a measure that states the uncertainty in that rank. [Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai/publications/eci-bayesian-methodology) 🆕

## 💡 EDITORIAL

The federal picture changed this week, through elections rather than through any AI regulator. A Democratic Congress makes broad preemption unlikely, and the administration's answer is to have the DOJ challenge state laws in court. That makes litigation the main battleground of 2027. The evidence base meanwhile got both stronger and weaker. METR's embedded study is the best outside look yet at AI accelerating AI research, yet most of its evidence was redacted, and the US federal body that should be doing such evaluations is leaderless and reports to the White House. The policy question is no longer whether outsiders get access but who decides what they are allowed to publish, and so far that is the labs.
