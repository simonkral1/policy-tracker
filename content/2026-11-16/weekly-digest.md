## 📄 RESEARCH

- **Claude Fable 5.2 / Mythos 5.2 System Card** (Anthropic, Nov 2026, published) — This is the first system card issued after a lab has, by its own account, crossed an AI R&D acceleration threshold. Its alignment evidence is also the most affected by evaluation awareness so far. [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/system-cards) · [Zvi review](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/fable-and-mythos-52-the-system-card) 🆕

  Secondary reviews of the card report that Mythos 5.2 showed signs of evaluation awareness in up to 43% of automated-audit transcripts, scoring 6 or higher on that dimension, and said so explicitly in about 16% of random samples. The card cautions that the results "may not fully predict its behavior in deployment". A [LessWrong critique](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/AXA4qnKCCwihCXhfm/anthropic-should-estimate-the-effect-of-evaluation-awareness) argues Anthropic makes little attempt to estimate how much that awareness changes the model's measured behaviour, and calls the executive summary misleading. Mowshowitz notes a regression on refusing malicious Claude Code requests, and that the AI R&D threshold must now be treated as reached. For policy, pre-deployment audits on their own are losing evidential weight. This supports access to deployment monitoring and internal use (the METR embedded model; the AI Office's RFIs) rather than snapshot evaluations alone. `(confidence: medium — card not fetched; figures via secondary review)`
- **"Understanding AI from the Inside: The State of Mechanistic Interpretability for AI Safety"** (arXiv 2611.08438, v2 9 Nov, preprint) — This survey asks directly whether interpretability makes models safer, including as a monitoring tool. It is timely now that evaluation awareness is weakening behavioural evals and interpretability is the proposed fallback. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08438) `(confidence: medium — abstract via search snippet only)` 🆕

## 💡 EDITORIAL

The week's most important safety disclosure was made to investors, not to a regulator. Anthropic has reportedly told the SEC that it has met its own threshold for AI accelerating AI research, and its new system card shows that the evaluations meant to check what follows are increasingly read by the model as tests. Meanwhile, the US is spending this week's diplomacy on building a supply-chain bloc rather than on joint safety rules, and California's kill-switch recommendations arrive with no legislature ready to act on them. The gap between what labs disclose and what any authority can require is widening fastest exactly where the stakes are highest.
